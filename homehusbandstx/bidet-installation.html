How the install works

  1. Shut off and drain The toilet's supply is closed at the stop valve and the tank drained so nothing spills.
  2. Fit the T-valve A tee goes in between the stop valve and the tank, giving the bidet its own feed off the same cold line. This is the whole reason no plumber is needed.
  3. Mount the unit The old seat comes off, the bidet's bracket goes on the existing bolt holes, and the seat slides into place and locks.
  4. Test under pressure Water back on, every joint checked, spray tested at full pressure, and the shutoff cycled to confirm it holds.
  5. Show you how it works Two minutes on the controls and the cleaning routine, then the packaging leaves with us.

Bidet installation questions

How much does it cost to install a bidet?

With us, a flat $149 including the bidet and supplies. For comparison, installation alone typically runs $100–$300 nationally before you buy the unit — so the bundled price removes the part most quotes leave out.

Do I need a plumber to install a bidet?

Not for a standard bidet seat or attachment. It tees off the toilet's existing supply line — no new plumbing, no cutting into walls, no permit. A licensed plumber is only needed for a standalone bidet fixture that requires its own drain, which is a different job entirely.

How long does it take?

About 45 minutes for a standard seat or attachment, start to finish, including the leak test.

Does a bidet need an electrical outlet?

A non-electric bidet does not — it runs on household water pressure alone. Heated-seat and warm-water models need a grounded outlet within reach of the toilet, ideally GFCI-protected. Most bathrooms built after the 1970s have one; some older Tomball homes do not. Tell us which model you want and we will check before we book.

Will a bidet fit my toilet?

Almost certainly. Bidet seats come in two shapes matching the two standard American bowl shapes. The measurement that matters is bowl length from the seat bolts to the front rim: roughly 18.5″ means elongated, roughly 16.5″ means round. Tell us which you have when you call, or just send a photo.

Can you install one on a wall-hung or one-piece toilet?

Usually yes. One-piece and skirted toilets sometimes need a different bracket or a longer supply hose, which we carry. Send a photo when you call so we confirm it beforehand instead of finding out on site.
