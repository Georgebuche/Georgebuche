Where they actually get in

On Houston-area homes it is almost always the same short list of gaps. A roof rat gets through a hole about the size of a quarter; a mouse needs less than a dime.

  • Roof-to-soffit junctions — where a lower roof meets a wall. The single most common way into an attic here.
  • Gable and turbine vents with missing or rusted-through screening.
  • Plumbing stacks and A/C line penetrations where the collar has perished or was never sealed.
  • Weep holes in brick veneer, unprotected.
  • Lifted flashing and ridge caps, often after a storm.
  • Garage door corners and dryer vents with failed flaps.

What we do

  1. Full walk-through Exterior and attic, photographed. You get shown every entry point we find, not just told a number.
  2. A quote based on what is actually there Home size, roof line and the number of openings. Priced once we have seen it, not guessed over the phone.

  4. Weep holes covered, not filled Stainless or plastic weep-hole covers that block pests while still letting the wall drain and breathe.
  5. A written list of what was closed Every point, with photos, so you know what was done and what to watch.

What we do not do

We seal. We do not trap, poison or remove live animals. If something is already living in your attic, it has to be out before the gaps close — and we will tell you that plainly and point you at a licensed wildlife control operator rather than taking the job.

Bats are a hard stop. They are protected in Texas, and sealing a roost while bats are inside is illegal as well as cruel. If we find bats we stop work and refer you to someone licensed to handle them under the proper exclusion timing.

Critter proofing questions

How much does critter proofing cost?

It depends on home size, roof line complexity and how many entry points we find, so it is quoted after a walk-through. A single-story with a simple roof is a very different job from a two-story with dormers and multiple rooflines — quoting either sight-unseen would be guessing, and you would end up with a "starting at" price that moves.

How do rats get into my attic?

Through gaps that are already there — roof-to-soffit junctions, unscreened vents, pipe penetrations, weep holes, lifted flashing. A roof rat needs a quarter-sized hole. They are not chewing their way through your roof; they are walking through an opening that has been there since the house was built or since the last storm.

What is exclusion work?

The construction side of a rodent problem: physically sealing the openings so it does not recur. Trapping handles the animals inside now. Exclusion stops the next ones. Trapping without exclusion means paying for repeat visits indefinitely.

Do you trap or remove animals?

No — sealing only. If there is anything live in the attic it needs to be out first, and we will say so and refer you on. Sealing an animal inside a wall creates a far worse problem than the one you called about.

What about bats?

Bats are protected in Texas. It is illegal to kill them or seal a roost shut while they are inside. We stop and refer you to a licensed operator who does legal bat exclusion at the right time of year.

Should weep holes be filled in?

No — never filled solid. Weep holes exist to let a brick-veneer wall drain and breathe, and sealing them causes moisture damage. The right fix is a stainless steel or plastic weep-hole cover that stops mice and roaches while keeping airflow and drainage.

Can you do this during a maintenance visit?

The walk-through, yes — our quarterly maintenance visit already includes an attic check for pest signs, and that is often how people find out. The sealing work itself is a separate, quoted job.
