The five-point checklist

1. A/C and vent filters changed

Filters swapped throughout the house. On the Gulf Coast the system runs most of the year, so filters load up faster than the packaging suggests — quarterly is the practical interval for most Tomball homes, monthly if you have pets or allergies. A loaded filter makes the system work harder, which shows up on the bill before it shows up as a breakdown.

2. Smoke alarm batteries replaced and every alarm tested

Every alarm gets a fresh battery and a live test. This is the item homeowners put off until an alarm chirps at 3am. Worth knowing: the alarm unit itself should be replaced entirely every ten years from its manufacture date — the sensor degrades even when the test button still works. We check the date stamps and tell you which ones are near the end.

3. A/C condensate drain line flushed

This is the one that saves a ceiling. The line carries condensate away from the evaporator coil, and in Houston humidity it runs wet nearly year-round, so algae and biofilm build up inside it. When it blocks, water backs up into the drain pan and then through the ceiling below. Flushing it quarterly costs a few minutes; not flushing it costs drywall, paint and an insurance claim.

4. Garbage disposal reset and checked

Run, drain, seal and mounting checked, and the reset tripped and cleared. Most "dead" disposals are a tripped overload, not a failure — worth confirming before anyone buys a new one.

5. Attic inspection — insulation, moisture and pest signs

The attic is where the three expensive problems start and where nobody looks. We check insulation depth and coverage (compressed or displaced insulation quietly raises cooling costs), look for moisture — roof leaks and sweating ducts stain the decking long before anything shows on a ceiling — and look for droppings, chewed insulation and rub marks that mean rodents are already in. Four looks a year catches all three while they are still cheap.

If the attic turns up rodent activity, that is what our critter proofing is for.

How often should I change my A/C filter in Texas?

Every one to three months. The system runs most of the year here, so a filter rated for three months in a milder climate loads faster. Quarterly suits most homes; monthly if you have pets or allergies.

Why does my A/C drain line keep clogging?

Because it stays wet. Year-round condensate plus Gulf Coast humidity lets algae grow inside the line. When it blocks, water goes into the pan and then into your ceiling. Quarterly flushing is the fix.

How often should smoke alarm batteries be replaced?

Batteries at least once a year, testing monthly. The alarm unit itself gets replaced every ten years from its manufacture date — the sensor degrades whether or not the test button still chirps.
